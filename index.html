01 — The Problem

Hardware failures rarely stay in one discipline.

A mechanical symptom can come from actuation, controls, electronics, tolerances, manufacturing or software. I work across the whole system to find the actual failure, not patch the symptom. Then I fix it and prove the fix with data.

Pick a symptom. See where the cause can hide.

Mechanism locks up

  1. Mechanics

    Over-constrained joints, misalignment, friction where a bearing should be.

    Root cause
  2. Actuation

    A motor that delivers less torque than its datasheet says.

    Second fault, caught on the bench
  3. Electronics

    Supply sag or a current limit tripping under load.

  4. Controls

    Limits and gains tuned for a different load.

  5. Manufacturing

    Tolerance stack-up, or parts out of spec.

Real case. A robot skeleton that locked itself. The cause was geometric binding in the mechanism. Bench-testing the replacement servos then showed about 60% of rated torque, a second fault that a 2.5× design margin absorbed. Read the SPM case study →

Position drifts

  1. Mechanics

    Cable, belt or screw geometry that changes length through the travel.

    Root cause
  2. Actuation

    Slip or missed steps under load.

  3. Electronics

    Sensor noise, grounding, a drifting reference.

  4. Controls

    Compensation that hides a hardware error.

    Symptom was patched here
  5. Manufacturing

    Assembly variation from one unit to the next.

Real case. A cable-driven concrete printer. The cable left a fixed spool at a changing angle, so its length changed. Software compensated, until the cable jumped a groove and every move after it was wrong. The fix was a redesigned spool, not better compensation. Read the case study →

Scrap is too high

  1. Mechanics

    Fixtures that do not locate parts repeatably.

  2. Actuation

    Force or stroke that varies from cycle to cycle.

  3. Electronics

    Sensors that do not measure what is critical to quality.

  4. Controls

    A process window narrower than the recipe assumes.

  5. Manufacturing

    Manual steps that vary by operator and shift.

Real case. A Class III implantable battery line running at about 30% scrap. Automating manual steps, retrofitting equipment and building CTQ data tools brought it to about 2%. Process details are confidential. See the result →

Parallel Robotics · Failure Recovery · UBC

Dead skeleton→working 3-DOF robot

Spherical parallel manipulator

Problem
Handed a self-locking robot skeleton: no motors, no electronics, no control system.
What I did
Rebuilt the mechanism on free bearings to remove the binding. Sized and bench-tested new servos (they measured ~60% of rated torque, and the 2.5× design margin absorbed it). Designed a fused 24→12 V power system, redesigned the mounts with FEA and fabricated everything at UBC Makerspaces.
Result
A fully controlled 3-DOF platform with potentiometer, desktop and Android control, IK-driven presets and an MSC Adams model.
  • Root-Cause Analysis
  • Actuator Validation
  • Power Protection
  • FEA
  • Raspberry Pi
  • Inverse Kinematics

Medical Devices · Prosthetics · ISO 13485

$30K+→<$3K

Affordable bionic prosthetic hand

Problem
Multi-grip bionic hands sell for $30K–60K, out of reach for most amputees in cost-sensitive markets.
What I did
Core mechanical designer: custom actuators, a 3D-printed body, CAE-driven design simplification and a patented thumb-swivel mechanism with position feedback, developed under ISO 13485.
Result
Priced under $3K, with 18 preset + 6 custom grip patterns and ~8 kg load capacity.
